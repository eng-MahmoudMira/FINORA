The challenge

Every customer raises three different questions

A bank has to read transactions and customers at the same time. Each question needs a different kind of intelligence — and a different model.

01 · Transaction

Is this transaction suspicious?

Fraud is rare and hides among thousands of legitimate payments. Every missed case is a loss for the bank and the customer.

02 · Relationship

Could this customer leave?

Customers rarely announce they are leaving. Knowing who is at risk early gives the bank time to act.

03 · Opportunity

Which product fits this customer?

Sending every offer to every customer costs money and annoys people. Relevance matters.

FINORA · Beyond the Numbers.

  • Stress that these are different analytical problems: a transaction-level signal, a relationship-level risk, and a product-level opportunity.
  • Transition: "So instead of one model trying to do everything, FINORA uses three — and connects them."

Part 1 · Business · Fraud detection

Catching the rare transaction that matters

The problem

Fraud is rare — about 1% of transactions — and gets lost among many legitimate ones. Every fraud we miss is a loss for the bank and the customer.

The solution

The fraud engine takes the transaction details — amount, merchant, time, customer and merchant location — and estimates the probability of fraud. If it crosses the saved threshold, the transaction is flagged for review.

FINORA · Beyond the Numbers.
  • 82% accuracy sounds good, but 80% of customers stay anyway — "nobody leaves" would score about the same.
  • The fix: lower the cut-off from 0.5 to 0.349. Recall rises from 0.49 to 0.63; precision falls from 0.74 to 0.65. We accept more false alarms to catch more leavers.
